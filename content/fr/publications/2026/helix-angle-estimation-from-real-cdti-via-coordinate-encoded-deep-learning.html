However, these rules assign the same transmural HA profile at every circumferential and longitudinal position, regardless of local anatomy – a limitation that real cDTI data could correct, though it remains costly to acquire. We investigate whether a 3D U-Net trained directly on a limited number of real cDTI hearts can capture the regional variations that this transmurally homogeneous rule cannot represent. LV geometries were expressed in a topology-agnostic ventricular coordinate system (φ, ρ, θ) (circumferential angle, transmural depth, longitudinal position), used both as a 3-channel network input and to project the target HA field onto a regular grid. The network was trained with a wrap-aware circular loss on real GT HA maps derived from cDTI eigenvector decomposition in a cohort of ovine and porcine hearts. This model, the Streeter rule, and a Streeter-pretrained-then-finetuned variant were all evaluated against real GT HA. On a held-out test set, our model achieved a mean angular error of 27.63◦ (median 20.99◦ ), compared to 45.40◦ (median 36.71◦ ) for the Streeter rule alone – a reduction of over 17◦ on average. The proposed approach – which closely matched the pretrained-then-finetuned variant – gives more accurate fiber orientation than the rule-based estimate alone, and could make subject-specific fiber modeling practical even when cDTI data is scarce. citation_full: >- Adrien Junior Tchuem Tchuente, Nicolas Cedilnik, Jairo Rodríguez Padilla, Mia Mojica, Julie Magat, et al..
